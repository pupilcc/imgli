# Commercial licensing

imgli’s public source is licensed under **[AGPL-3.0-only](LICENSE)**.

That is intentional: anyone can self-host, study, and improve the software, but
network use of modified versions carries AGPL source-sharing obligations. This
reduces “closed SaaS / white-label freeloading” of the open codebase.

## When you need a commercial license

You typically need a **paid commercial license** (separate written agreement)
if you cannot or do not want to comply with the AGPL, for example:

- You modify imgli and offer it as a **network service** without publishing
  your corresponding source under AGPL.
- You distribute a **proprietary** product that includes imgli (or substantial
  portions) without AGPL terms applying to the combination as required.
- Your legal / compliance team requires a **non-copyleft** license for internal
  redistribution or OEM embedding.
- You want **contractual support, indemnity, or enterprise terms** beyond the
  AGPL’s “as-is” disclaimer.

Self-hosting an **unmodified** release for your own users, while complying with
AGPL for any modifications you make and convey, usually does **not** require a
commercial license. When in doubt, ask.

## What a commercial license is (and is not)

| Commercial license | Not included unless contracted |
|--------------------|--------------------------------|
| Permission to use / modify / distribute under negotiated terms instead of AGPL for the covered code | Unlimited free support |
| Clarity for proprietary or multi-tenant products | Ownership transfer of the OSS project |
| Optional support / update / SLA riders | Obligation for us to accept every feature request |

Pricing, seat / instance metrics, and term length are defined in the order form
or enterprise agreement — not in this file.

## Dual licensing model

- **Community:** AGPL-3.0-only (this repository, default)
- **Commercial:** proprietary license from the copyright holder, covering the
  versions and components listed in the agreement

External contributions are accepted only under the project’s contributor terms
(see [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md)) so dual licensing remains possible.
